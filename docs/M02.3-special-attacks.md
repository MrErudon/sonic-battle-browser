# M02.3 — Configurable special attacks

## Baseline and authorization
Baseline: main `75fd73c104ff28fa8e3ed88acf65bcede261352c` (merged PR #8).
Master approved this numerical design and architecture. Human M02.1/M02.2 and
special balance testing remain pending. These are prototype values, not measured
original GBA data. No roster/customization, aerial specials, combo cancels,
special counters, healing, multiplayer or hosting changes are included.

## Controls and action rules
1 selects Power Burst; 2 Dash Strike; 3 Energy Projectile; I activates.
Selection buttons also work. Selection changes never interrupt actions.
I captures the selected ID on keydown, survives zero-tick renders, is consumed
once and never auto-repeats. Changing selection later cannot change a queued ID.
Illegal requests are discarded, with no recharge cost or delayed replay.

Priority is legal held block, existing jump/normal attack, then special.
J/K retain their first-edge contract and six-tick combo buffer; both beat I
regardless of event order. Jump+I jumps without a special. Direction+I selects
the same eight screen-relative directions without walking that tick.
Facing then locks; no steering or auto-aim.

Activation requires grounded Ready, no landing/hitstun/guard break, zero general
cooldown, available individual/shared recharge, and (for Energy) no live owned
projectile. Specials cannot cancel any action, including attack recovery.
Received damage interrupts startup, active or recovery without refund.
Emitted projectiles survive owner hitstun; interruption before emission prevents
spawn. Contacts committed on the emission tick still trade with incoming hits.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Startup / active / recovery | 15 / 4 / 24 | 9 / 8 / 22 | 18 / 1 / 20 |
| Damage / hitstun | 20 / 26 | 12 / 18 | 10 / 14 |
| Individual recharge | 120 | 150 | 180 |
| Guard damage / blockstun | 35 / 10 | 20 / 6 | 15 / 6 |
| Knockback / launch | 360 / 200 | 180 / 0 | 120 / 0 |
| Reach / width | 85 / 64 | 32 / 32 during travel | Orb radius 6 |
| Vertical interval | 4–48 | 6–40 | 16–28 |
| Travel | None | 110 over eight active ticks | Speed 260/s; maximum 300 |
| Projectile lifetime | — | — | 70 movement ticks |

Shared special lockout: 60 ticks. General post-action cooldown: four ticks.
Power phases are 1–15 / 16–19 / 20–43, completion on frame 44.
Dash phases are 1–9 / 10–17 / 18–39, completion on frame 40.
Energy phases are 1–18 / emission 19 / recovery 20–39, completion on frame 40.

Recharge begins on accepted activation, not contact or completion. At activation
tick T, a recharge C becomes eligible at T+C. Decrement runs before special
eligibility; rejected requests do not charge. Individual/shared recharge never
locks normal combat. The separate general cooldown retains the old final-tick
control lock: its transition to zero does not accept an attack/block/special
until the next tick. Movement remains available during general cooldown.

Configuration is validated and deeply copied/frozen. Defaults are shared
immutable data, not repeated in each mutable fighter snapshot. Custom
FighterStats.specials snapshots are independent of caller mutations.
Validators reject missing definitions/fields, wrong IDs, invalid or nonfinite
durations/geometry, unsafe terminal recovery, and insufficient projectile life.

## Simulation architecture
- specials.ts: configuration, action/runtime types, recharge and eligibility.
- collision.ts: segment versus rounded oriented rectangle/circle, relative
  target movement, vertical overlap and first arena-boundary intersection.
- contacts.ts: one shared defensive-snapshot contact resolver.
- projectiles.ts: persistent CombatState and finite simulation-owned entities.
- combat.ts: existing normal rules plus special phases, dash planning, spawning
  and shared contact collection.
- MatchController owns CombatState and passes it to combat/CPU. Legacy two-argument
  stepCombat remains a normal-only adapter; requesting specials without explicit
  persistent state throws rather than silently losing entities.

Instance IDs use owner ID plus an owner-local sequence. Projectile ID is its
originating special ID. No randomness in collision/damage; no Phaser, timers,
hidden world state or global RNG in the core.

## Dash collision
Unobstructed movement is exactly 13.75 units on frames 10–17.
Each tick plans straight travel, clips it to the first footprint boundary,
then sweeps the oriented attack volume against relative target movement.
Horizontal rounded corners are checked without inflating them into squares.
Vertical overlap uses linear interpolation of the simulation tick endpoints.

The earliest contact stops the dash at its actual contact time. Directional
guard uses those interpolated positions before passing the defender. Both dash
paths are planned before resolution. Later normal/projectile contacts see the
clipped endpoints. Remaining active/recovery ticks continue without further
dash travel. Walls stop travel without sliding, reflection or bounces.

This is 60 Hz swept special collision, not a new swept model for ordinary
attacks or a multiplayer physics solver. Simultaneous active contacts collected
this tick still trade; no armor/invulnerability is added.

## Projectile lifecycle
Exactly one orb spawns at the owner's center, z=22, on frame 19.
Initial target overlap is checked that tick; movement starts next tick.
Each full step is 260/60 units. After 69 steps travel is 299; the final step is
clamped to one unit before expiration at 300. Walls use projectile radius.
The first target contact consumes the orb even if blocked. Owner is excluded.
Projectiles pass through each other; no clashes, reflection or destruction by
normal attacks. Maximum one per owner, two total in this two-fighter match.
An attempt to cast a second live owned orb is rejected without cost.

Projectile collision sweeps relative target motion and vertical overlap, not
sprite pixels. If a wall clips a tick, target movement is evaluated over the
same fraction of the tick. Stable IDs resolve exact target-distance ties.

## Defensive and simultaneous contacts
Burst/Dash use frontal guard. Projectile guard uses incoming velocity, so moving
the owner behind the defender cannot invalidate a frontal block.
Default chip is ceil(damage*.25): 5 / 3 / 3, lethal and HP-clamped.
Blocked knockback uses the defender's existing ratio; blocked hits do not launch.
Breaking contact chips; the guard-break timer remains 36, never refreshed by
later incoming damage. Ordinary hitstun can outlast refill and still completes.

Gather every contact before applying reactions. Each contact snapshots its
block eligibility, including contact-time positions for Dash.
For each defender:
1. Process contacts in stable instance/target-ID order for event allocation.
2. Aggregate damage and guard depletion from that same defensive snapshot.
   All contacts classified blocked chip, even if their combined guard cost
   exhausts guard. Only the first exhausting contact reports guard_break.
3. Apply one HP/guard/reaction transaction. Event damage sums to actual HP loss.
4. Use maximum incoming full-hit stun/launch; strongest horizontal knockback
   wins, with stable ID ties. Full-hit stun takes priority over break pose,
   while the independent break timer continues.
5. KO cannot be overwritten by a later chip/block reaction.

After the complete batch, MatchController adjudicates KO/double KO.
Any KO clears live projectiles. Opposing lethal attacks/projectiles still trade.

Only normal unblocked contacts confirm a normal chain. Special hits never add
normal confirmations or increment the normal hit counter. Contact sets prevent
repeat damage; blocked projectiles are consumed too.

## CPU
Special probabilities/reaction ticks:
Easy .10/24, Normal .25/12, Hard .40/8.
A separate seeded stream isolates special choices from normal/defensive RNG.
No decision mutates fighter state; only legal inputs are emitted.
Priority: existing combo continuation, defense, special opportunity, normal
offense/pursuit. Failed rolls wait an ordinary attack interval; no per-tick
rerolls or initiation interval bypass.
Bands along current committed facing: Burst 0–70, Dash >70–140, Energy >140–280.
Lateral/height/recharge checks apply. CPU does not turn for free, predict inputs,
guarantee contact, or change stats. Current visible specials and already-emitted
projectiles extend existing delayed defense observation. Incoming projectiles
use their path rather than owner position.

Callers omitting CombatState retain the pre-special CPU decision path, enabling
normal-only regression comparison. Production MatchController always passes it.

## Pause, boundaries and visuals
Pause/help freezes simulation time, accepted special actions, projectiles and
recharge. It clears queued I/selection key activity; repeats cannot rearm it.
Rounds/rematch reset all recharge and entities; selection persists.
Menu resets selection to Burst. KO clears pending inputs, special actions and
entities. Scene shutdown removes listeners and destroys effect objects.

Original SpecialView indicators read phases/positions only. Effect base IDs:
special_power, special_dash, special_projectile, with startup/active/recovery
suffixes. Energy orb, dash trail and impact colors are original procedural art.
CombatView consumes specialId/source/outcome events; replacing art cannot
change collision. Special selection/recharge/shared-lockout feedback is a
compact separate control strip, preserving existing health/guard layout.

23 SHA-256 traces / 25,200 ticks, covering three CPU presets, five seeds and all
eight directional scripted normal/air/combo/guard interactions. The checked-in
goldens have no regeneration switch. Snapshot excludes only new special state
and identifiers; all old fighter/action fields and ordered hit payloads remain.

Existing tests stay in place. New tests cover exact phases/recharge/general
cooldown, invalid activation, input ordering, interruptions, all eight dash
directions, wall/corner/tangent cases, moving target sweeps, projectile spawn/
travel/life/owner exclusion, simultaneous guards/lethal trades, live projectile
plus normal contacts, double KO, CPU legality/replay/ranges/frequency, real
pressure escape, match cleanup and 10/30/60/144/240 Hz equivalence.

Compiled Chromium/Firefox/WebKit use shipped keyboard inputs and public HUD/
Debug to select/cast/connect every special, observe an orb in flight, verify
recharge/pause/reset feedback and retain all previous combo/guard/match/layout
checks. No health/position/score/controller mutations in production playthroughs.
Use the PR's exact-head CI results/artifacts for final outcomes.

Human gameplay/balance remains pending. Ground-only specials and procedural
art are intentional limits. Browser matrix is desktop-engine automation, not
real-device/mobile play or performance certification. Large Phaser bundle
