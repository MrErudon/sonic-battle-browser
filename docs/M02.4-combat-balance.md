## Architecture and exact decision values

`cpu.ts` is preserved byte-for-byte. `cpu-observation.ts` ages visible action/projectile instances and filters threats by current geometry, height, remaining travel and a 30-tick projectile path horizon. `cpu-policy.ts` issues ordinary `CombatInput`; it cannot change fighter statistics, collision, action restrictions or contact resolution. No future input or enemy RNG is read. All decisions use local seeded streams; offense, defense, special opportunities and tactical punish rolls have separate streams.

| Parameter (ticks at 60 Hz) | Easy | Normal | Hard |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Ordinary reaction (unchanged) | 24 | 12 | 5 |
| Defense / special observation age (unchanged) | 24 | 12 | 8 |
| Attack interval (unchanged) | 70 | 40 | 24 |
| Initial special probability (unchanged) | 10% | 25% | 40% |
| Punish probability per eligible instance | 15% | 40% | 70% |
| Preferred positioning band (arena units) | 45–75 | 40–70 | 35–65 |
| Positioning center | 60 | 55 | 50 |
| Movement commitment maximum | 8 | 6 | 4 |
| Projectile sidestep burst maximum | 6 | 8 | 10 |
| Defense retry delay | 24 | 18 | 12 |
| Existing maximum guard hold | 24 | 36 | 48 |

Positioning uses the upper band plus 6 units of hysteresis and the center as its stopping target. Attack geometry can require approaching inside that band; these are preferences, not artificial collision limits. A commitment is discarded if it cannot make progress at a wall. The M01.4 close-overlap facing correction and vertical wait remain; its close correction is restricted to 44 units to prevent alternating retreat/approach at wider spacing.

Priority: legal combo continuation, ongoing guard/avoidance, aged threat reaction, recovery punish, low-guard heavy pressure, special opportunity, facing/positioning, ordinary offense. Punishes require six elapsed recovery observations, the ordinary reaction window, no ordinary attack-interval bypass, and enough visible recovery for startup plus a 3-tick margin. One roll is allowed per opponent action instance. Quiet guard releases after six observations. Special bands are Burst 0–70, Dash >70–140, Projectile >140–280 units, with facing/lateral/height legality and existing individual recharge/shared lockout checks. Guard ≤45 permits legal heavy pressure; it does not alter guard damage.

Three missed contacts within a rolling 120-tick window suppress that move kind for 60 ticks. Interrupted actions count as failures for policy memory; telemetry distinguishes interruptions from completed whiffs. Memory and responded-instance sets are bounded. Controller replacement on round/menu/rematch lifecycle clears memory. Pausing/focus loss does not age observations or commitments because the simulation stops.

## Passive telemetry and baseline provenance

`CombatTelemetry` is development-only: no analytics services, storage, network, clocks or RNG. Accepted attack callbacks receive frozen scalar DTOs; hit events carry attack kind and instance ID. These additions do not alter resolution. The browser does not import the telemetry implementation. Metrics include accepted attempts, contacts, completed whiffs, interruptions, full/chip damage by kind, blocks, guard breaks, guard consumption/regeneration/refill, action occupancy and decision reasons. Round duration is measured by the duel harness; full-match duration is measured separately. Guard deltas are net per-tick changes; unresolved attacks at KO are not retroactively marked completed whiffs.

The original legacy baseline was recorded **before implementing the refined decisions**, and is preserved at `reports/M02.4-baseline.json`. A later measurement review found that schema 1 omitted special acceptance notifications. That report is retained unchanged, rather than silently repaired. Schema 2 compares **both policies using complete special accounting**. All original baseline combat aggregates reproduce exactly; the original whiff ratio is excluded from that reproduction comparison because its attempt accounting was incomplete. The schema-2 legacy column below is the valid paired comparator.

Telemetry on/off tests cover both policies, all three difficulties and five seeds. They compare every input, fighter/world/event/decision trace and the actual `Math.imul` operands/results from seeded RNG calls. Rendering-rate tests compare complete refined traces at 10/30/60/144/240 Hz. No telemetry object is used to choose an input.

## Paired simulation methodology

100 seeds (0–99), three difficulties, eight adversarial scripts, four cardinal orientations and mirrored starts: **19,200 identical fixtures per policy**, 38,400 duels. Scripts: idle, held light, held heavy, combo pressure, held directional guard, repeated projectiles, rotating specials/light and corner pressure. Inputs are adversarial schedules submitted to ordinary simulation legality; some scripts intentionally submit fresh attack edges every tick. Each duel ends at KO or is censored at 10,800 fighting ticks. A winner is counted only for uncensored duels; double KO is a draw. The light and combo schedules currently coincide, so their results are correlated rather than independent evidence of distinct player styles.

The holdout uses seeds 100–124: **4,800 fixtures per policy**. It confirms seed generalization after the pursuit fix, but uses the same scenario family and is not a human validation set. The full-match harness uses five seeds, three difficulties and idle/pressure/projectile schedules for **90 matches across both policies**, including countdowns and round transitions, capped at 72,000 ticks each. Both policies use identical combat configuration and corresponding seeds in every comparison. Tactical policy stream consumption can differ; fairness means identical initial seeds, not identical RNG draws after different decisions.

Mean damage taken falls **4.97%**, below the 20% gate. Whiff rate increases from 0.247% to 1.047%; the reduction gate fails. The corrected denominator includes specials; interruptions are separate. A higher victory count does not establish a globally better policy.

Projectile-only fixtures improve mean damage taken **36.79 → 11.658 (−68.31%)**, median duration **669 → 556 ticks**, and victories **2252 → 2400 / 2400**. These pass both projectile gates. Received guard breaks increase **332 → 616** over the primary suite; net guard refill increases **30,000 → 51,200**. Better projectile avoidance does not imply better defense against normal pressure. No guard, hitstun or damage values were adjusted in response.

## Limits and next review

The scripts cannot establish subjective fairness, readable animations or human exploit resistance. CPU mirror matches, moving human zoning and unbounded adversarial search are outside this evaluation. The new policy sees currently visible fighter state, including guard/recovery and projectile geometry; it does not receive user controls or future state. Its wider positioning bands yield to actual attack geometry, so preferred distance is not guaranteed. Exact thresholds and timing remain provisional CPU policy choices; combat tuning is preserved.

Independent review should concentrate on the increased received guard breaks, remaining no-progress flags, missed whiff/damage gates, Easy/Normal separation, observation timing and default-policy rollout. Human M02.1–M02.3 testing remains pending. Merge, deployment, hosting changes and substantial multiplayer work are outside this PR.
